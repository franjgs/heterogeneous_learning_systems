# Targeted literature search: adaptive advantage under changing conditions

**Date:** 2026-10-19
**Decision:** **STOP** — no candidate is yet suitable for authorization as a faithful HLS reproduction of an adaptive organizational strategy outperforming a strong fixed organizational strategy in a genuinely changing environment. This is a bounded targeted search, not a systematic review.

This report extends rather than repeats the [October 19 literature-selection review](HLS_LITERATURE_REPRODUCTION_SELECTION_2026-10-19.md). That review covered six registered sources and found no reproduction-ready candidate. Here the search target was specifically a published, positive adaptive-versus-fixed comparison. No simulations, code changes, or HLS result changes were made. HLS Laboratory remains **PARTIAL**.

Labels used below: **FACT** is directly reported by a primary source or its official artifact; **INFERENCE** is our interpretation; **PROPOSAL** is a possible later action, not authorization.

## Search method and selection rule

**FACT.** I screened peer-reviewed primary studies found through targeted searches combining “adaptive/fixed”, “non-stationary/dynamic”, “online learning”, and “allocation/scheduling”. I then checked candidate papers themselves, their figures/tables/equations, and publisher/author artifact links. The three candidates retained for comparison are Mettler, Massey & Kellman (2016), Cavenaghi et al. (2021), and Cheung, Simchi-Levi & Zhu (2022). Search was not exhaustive and does not establish these are the only candidates.

**INFERENCE.** A candidate must have (i) a measurable change in the decision-relevant environment or learner state, (ii) an adaptive decision rule using feedback, (iii) a nontrivial fixed comparator, and (iv) enough primary evidence/artifacts to reproduce the stated experiment. “Fixed” may mean fixed action/policy or fixed algorithm; these are not interchangeable. A stationary-learning algorithm can adapt its action to rewards while still failing to adapt its model to drift.

## Evidence and interpretation

### Closest source for actual environmental drift: Cavenaghi et al.

**FACT.** The environment is a nonstationary Bernoulli bandit: at each round one of four arms is selected; only that arm’s reward is observed; reward means change abruptly or incrementally. For random scenarios, initial arm means and replacement means are sampled uniformly on [0,1], and each arm has drift probability d; 1,000 environments are averaged per scenario (Methods §4.2.1). The custom scenarios use four arms, 1,000 rounds and 100 runs (Methods §4.2.2). The adaptive mechanism discounts older evidence and separately keeps the last n outcomes per arm; its variants aggregate historic and recent posterior samples with f ∈ {min, max, mean} (Algorithm 1).

**FACT.** Fig. 9 is the most directly positive controlled case: on its last 450 rounds, standard TS is described as failing after the final change; the f-dsw family adapts, with min-dsw having lowest regret after the second change and mean-dsw highest overall cumulative reward after the last change. The paper’s Fig. 10 increasing case is an important negative/control case: min-dsw can have regret slightly above TS, Discounted TS has best cumulative reward, and max-dsw performs best after changes. The stationary Fig. 11 also shows the cost of drift adaptation when rewards do not change (§5.2). This context dependence argues against a universal superiority claim.

**FACT.** Figure 2a defines the custom-decreasing sequence explicitly: four arm means start at (0.9, 0.7, 0.1, 0.3); arm 1 drops to 0 at step 250 and arm 2 drops to 0 at step 500. That makes arms 3/4 the available alternatives after changes and ensures the best action changes. The paper’s formulation is Bernoulli reward with unknown time-indexed means; policy input is only the prior and selected-arm reward history (Eqs. 2–7). For the custom case, the main text does not give a complete per-algorithm hyperparameter table; §4.4 gives tuning for random and real-data settings, and exact custom-case settings must be recovered from the linked implementation before a faithful rerun.

**FACT.** On real data, Table 4 reports means over ten replicates and Appendix A reports boxplots. For Insects-abrupt, min-dsw accuracy is 40.24%, versus 39.94% for Sliding-Window TS and 28.80% for stationary TS; the margin over the strongest listed baseline is 0.30 percentage points. For Insects-incremental, min-dsw is 40.47%, versus 39.94% mean-dsw and 39.72% SW-TS. Local News is described as pseudo-stationary; min-dsw 53.70% is 2.21 points above TS at 51.49%, but aggressive drift adaptation is not uniformly helpful. These Table 4 means are not accompanied there by confidence intervals or a reported between-policy significance test. “Best mean” must not be rewritten as “statistically established advantage.” [Primary article, Tables 3–4, Figs. 7–11 and Appendix A](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8004723/); [linked code](https://github.com/CavenaghiEmanuele/Multi-armed-bandit).

**INFERENCE.** This is a real environmental nonstationarity benchmark, but its arm identity represents a reward distribution/classifier action, not an HLS worker or task allocation. The source is reproducible in principle outside HLS; it is not directly reproducible with the currently validated HLS N×K, Nembhard, or contextual-router mechanisms. The paper’s adaptive advantage is “drift-aware posterior management vs methods that do not manage drift as effectively,” not “dynamic organizational reassignment beats a globally optimized fixed organizational design.”

The source also does not report a significance test or uncertainty interval for the custom-case Fig. 9 means. Moreover, the paper says its empirical methods are compared with tuned parameters and uses parameter-selection sets for several scenarios (§4.4, Tables 2–3). The source code and the exact custom-run configuration therefore matter; reading values off the plot would not be an adequate reproduction target.

### Strongest fixed comparison: Mettler et al.

**FACT.** Experiment 2’s yoking is a rigorous control for the overall distribution of intervals and, in the yoked-item arm, item-specific intervals copied from a previous adaptive participant. The main positive results are change scores rather than a uniform result on every measure: adaptive versus yoked-item is significant immediately but not at one week; adaptive versus yoked-random is significant at both. The source also notes alternative gain-score comparisons were weaker (immediate p=.057/.058; delayed adaptive vs yoked-item p=.12). The outcome is recall accuracy, not production, throughput, or organizational utility. The source states learning strength varies dynamically with learner and item, and the interval rule uses ongoing correctness/RT; it is not an exogenous demand-shift experiment. [Primary article, Experiment 2, Results and Discussion; Fig. 10 and Appendix Table A1](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000170).

**INFERENCE.** This is persuasive evidence that online item-level feedback can outperform a fixed schedule matched to adaptive schedules in a human learning task. It is the best example found of protecting against a deliberately weak fixed baseline. Yet it cannot validate HLS adaptive coordination: the changing state is human memory/recall, the action is retrieval spacing, and there is no heterogeneous-agent assignment problem.

### Cheung et al.

**FACT.** The paper’s nonstationary model evaluates regret relative to time-varying best actions; the auto-loan dataset contains 50,000 applications spanning 276 days. Fig. 5 reports that SW-UCB with known variation budget and BOB have the lowest dynamic regret among plotted methods. The article describes simulation on synthetic data and loan data; it does not establish a fixed-allocation comparator. [Primary Management Science source](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2021.4024), §9/Fig. 5; [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.01461).

**INFERENCE.** This is a useful adaptive-control benchmark, but an adaptive-vs-fixed advantage would require a new contrast the published paper does not report, and its pricing context is not an organizational allocation model. That contrast must not be attributed retroactively to the paper.

## Reproduction and HLS compatibility boundary

| Requirement | Cavenaghi Fig. 9 | Mettler Experiment 2 | Cheung §9/Fig. 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Actual changing external reward/demand process | **Yes**, arm reward means drift | **No**; changing endogenous learner/item strength | **Yes**, changing demand/reward model |
| Adaptive policy uses observed feedback | Yes, selected-arm reward | Yes, item correctness and RT | Yes, contextual payoff feedback |
| Strong fixed comparator | TS is a credible stationary-learning baseline; not fixed allocation; additional drift-aware baselines | **Yes**, adaptive-derived yoked fixed schedules | Stationary UCB is a credible algorithm baseline; historical company decisions are not a strong fixed policy |
| Exact positive number plus uncertainty | Fig. 9 qualitative ranking; 100-run averages, no inferential interval reported | Exact means, SDs, test statistics and effect sizes; result varies by measure/time | Dynamic-regret curves; no single tabulated contrast against an optimized fixed policy established here |
| Primary reproducibility artifacts | Synthetic generator + linked code; must pin code/version and random protocol | Human task/participants; no study code/raw data verified | Author preprint; data reconstruction/input access and code/version remain to verify |
| Direct HLS reuse | Low: HLS lacks nonstationary reward posterior/window model | Very low: HLS has no human memory/RT process | Low: HLS lacks price-response model and variation-budget optimization |

**FACT.** HLS already has exogenous stationary/nonstationary/Markov demand and state-responsive, estimator-based, and full-information N×K routes, as recorded in the [canonical capability validation](HLS_LAB_ADAPTIVE_CAPABILITY_VALIDATION.md). This does not implement the reward-drift bandit, ARTS memory process, or nonstationary contextual pricing model.

Concrete HLS evidence is in the environment trajectory and execution contracts (src/hls/hls_lab_nk_environment.py and src/hls/hls_lab_nk_execution.py; tests/test_hls_lab_nk_environment.py), N×K decision policies (src/hls/hls_lab_nk_policies.py; tests/test_hls_lab_nk_policies.py), Nembhard adaptive estimator/router (src/hls/hls_lab_nk_nembhard_adaptive.py; tests/test_hls_lab_nk_nembhard_adaptive.py), and the contextual quality-cost router (src/hls/contextual_router.py; tests/test_hls_contextual_router.py). These are adjacent facilities, not equivalent implementations of the candidate papers.

**INFERENCE.** An external reimplementation of a source model could reproduce a paper while testing none of HLS’s existing organizational mechanisms. Conversely, mapping arms to agents or rewards to production would alter source semantics and be a conceptual adaptation, not reproduction. A paper equation or available code alone does not make its article reproducible in HLS.

## Recommendation and decision gate

**Finding:** the search found published positive adaptive results, but no one candidate simultaneously supplies (i) genuine exogenous drift, (ii) a strong fixed *strategy* comparator, (iii) precise positive numerical evidence with suitable uncertainty, and (iv) an identifiable reproduction path using existing HLS semantics.

**Ranked candidates:**

1. **Cavenaghi et al. (2021), Fig. 9 custom-decreasing** — best target if CE accepts stationary TS as a strong *non-drift-adaptive algorithm* baseline. It has actual reward-distribution drift, a mechanism tied to forgetting/recent evidence, 100 controlled replications and author code. Before any later reproduction, recover and freeze the source code commit, exact random seeds/environment trajectories, parameter selection procedure, and uncertainty/statistical analysis. Do not call this a fixed-allocation comparison.
2. **Mettler et al. (2016), Experiment 2** — best fixed-comparator and reported-statistics reference; not a dynamic external environment, not an HLS organizational task, and requires a human experiment. Use only if the research question is explicitly reframed around feedback-responsive scheduling of a changing learner state.
3. **Cheung et al. (2022), §9/Fig. 5** — strong nonstationary optimization reference with a real temporal dataset, but no published optimized fixed-allocation comparison and unresolved data/code reconstruction dependencies.

**PROPOSAL.** Do not authorize implementation or describe any candidate as the selected HLS reproduction yet. Ask CE to decide whether the next target should prioritize (a) true exogenous drift and computational reproducibility (Cavenaghi; stationary-algorithm baseline), or (b) the strongest matched fixed schedule and statistically reported human learning outcome (Mettler; endogenous learner dynamics). If the requirement remains “adaptive work allocation beats an optimized fixed allocation under changing external demand,” the defensible result of this search is **none identified**; widen the targeted search or accept that an exact source comparison has not yet been found.

**Final recommendation: STOP for reproduction authorization.** This is not a negative finding about adaptive methods. It is a finding that the closest positive sources test materially different contrasts. A later protocol should state which notion of “dynamic” and “fixed strategy” is intended before selecting a source. No implementation, experiment, or campaign is authorized by this report.

# HLS theory: normalized concepts and scoped results

## 1. Scientific scope

Heterogeneous Learning Systems (HLS) studies how different capabilities are distributed, organized, used, combined, and developed to solve problems under constraints. The scientific responsibilities are **scenarios**, **organizations**, **models**, and **performance evaluation**. They are linked concepts, not a universal formalism or required software modules. A model is an explicit representation used to answer a question; it is not a scenario or an experiment.

No algorithm, application, analytic decomposition, or named study defines HLS. SOC and LLM systems are possible application areas.

## 2. Normalized vocabulary and notation

The following notation is a translation convention. A model may refine it or decline a mapping if the source concepts do not match.

| Term | Normalized meaning | Boundary / non-equivalence |
|---|---|---|
| Agent | An entity that can receive, perform, or contribute to work. | Does not imply autonomy, cognition, or a human/AI identity. |
| Capability | A system-level or agent-level set/relation describing what outcomes are attainable under specified conditions. | Not a scalar score or an observed success rate by default. |
| Competence | A state-dependent disposition or parameter governing performance on a specified task under stated conditions. | Distinct from capability, productivity, accuracy, and a particular outcome. A model must state its operational link. |
| Task / problem | A unit or class of demand with a required outcome and, where applicable, type, arrival, reward, deadline, or resource need. | A task family is not itself a scenario. |
| Environment | Exogenous conditions and stochastic mechanisms governing demand, outcomes, and resource availability. | Environment changes need not be caused by the organization. |
| State | Information sufficient for the model's transition/evaluation at a time. | May include competence, queues, beliefs, or resources; state variables are model-specific. |
| Organization | The arrangement of agents, roles, access, and coordination constraints that determines feasible collective action. | Not the policy that selects an action. |
| Policy | A mapping from allowed observations/history to decisions. | Not synonymous with organization: the same policy can operate under different structures, and one structure can admit many policies. |
| Decision / action | A choice made at a decision point, including assignment, cooperation, training, or resource use when modeled. | An action is not a result or a policy. |
| Resource | A consumable or capacity-limited quantity with a declared unit (e.g. time, compute, budget, worker slots). | Do not aggregate unlike units without an explicit conversion. |
| Constraint | A feasibility condition on states, actions, outcomes, or resource use. | A deadline, capacity limit, and information restriction are distinct constraint types. |
| Cost | A measured or modeled undesirable resource/outcome, with unit and accounting boundary. | Operational resource cost differs from compute time used by the simulator. |
| Performance | A declared outcome measure or vector, population, horizon, and aggregation rule. | Individual quality is not collective performance; statistical significance is not practical importance. |
| Experience | An observation/exposure generated by participation in an event or task. | Experience is not learning or competence change. |
| Learning | A change in a competence-relevant state caused by experience or intervention, represented by a transition. | Must be distinguished from policy adaptation. |
| Adaptation | A change in decisions or policy parameters in response to observations/context. | Can occur with fixed competencies; does not imply learning. |
| Cooperation | Multiple agents jointly contribute to the same task or outcome under a specified protocol. | Not merely routing among agents or combining independent predictions. |
| Specialization | A structured variation in competence/capability across task dimensions relative to an explicitly stated reference. | It is not a scalar virtue; its effect depends on demand and organization. |
| Uncertainty | A specified distribution or incomplete information about environment, state, outcomes, or parameters. | Aleatory variation and epistemic uncertainty should not be conflated. |

### Common mathematical shell

For a model that admits this mapping, let agents be (i\in\mathcal A), tasks (x\in\mathcal X), time (t\in\mathcal T), environment (e_t), state (s_t), organization (o\in\mathcal O), policy \(\pi\), decision \(a_t\), and resource vector \(r_t\). A policy acts on an allowed observation/history \(h_t\) and must choose from a feasible set:

\[
 a_t\sim\pi(\cdot\mid h_t),\qquad a_t\in\mathcal F(o,s_t,e_t).
\]

A model-specific outcome mechanism gives \(Y_t\sim P(\cdot\mid s_t,e_t,a_t)\), and a dynamic model may specify
\[
 s_{t+1}=F(s_t,a_t,e_t,\xi_t),
\]
where \(\xi_t\) is an explicitly defined disturbance. Performance is a model-declared functional \(J_\theta(\pi;o)\) (or a vector), under horizon/population \(\theta\). This notation is a semantic bridge, not an assertion that every HLS study has a common scalar objective or a Markov state.

When a competence parameter is probabilistic, use \(q_i(x\mid s,e)=P(\text{specified success}\mid i,x,s,e)\), with event and conditioning stated. It is not “capability” itself. In a deterministic model, competence may instead be a vector or map; do not force it into a probability.

Units remain model-specific. Probabilities and normalized effectiveness are dimensionless; static resource coordinates are declared model units and are not convertible to seconds, compute, or money without an explicit mapping. Queue service and deadlines use time units from their scenario. Aggregates must retain their population, horizon, denominator, and units.

### Historical mapping limits

- The static foundational formulation maps competence state \(S\), demand \(P\), constraints \(B\), and policy class \(\Pi\) to feasible effectiveness/resource outcomes. Its \(S\) is a model-specific state, not a universal definition of competence.
- The dynamic foundational formulation uses an endogenous action-dependent transition. A changing competence state alone is not learning unless the model ties it to action/experience.
- In the deadline-queue model, q(i,k) is a conditional correctness probability; assignment policies use load, known correctness, estimated correctness, or contextual adaptation. The matched-marginal structure comparison changes the joint competence matrix. Those quantities do not map to every finite competence-state formulation.
- A sequential computation-selection model’s internal state, computation invocation, and physical task reward are specific to sequential metareasoning; no one-to-one mapping to worker competence or organizational capability is established.
- Historical USE/DEVELOP labels may refer to actions in a particular model. There is no valid universal mapping of “develop” to learning or “use” to operation without the model's transition and observation rules.

## 3. Scenario, organization, model, study, experiment, result

A **scenario** fixes the problem/demand/environmental conditions and constraints. An **organization** fixes the available entities, competence structure, roles/access and coordination possibilities. A **model** states the objects, equations, transition/outcome laws and assumptions. **Evaluation** specifies estimands, metrics, comparators, sampling units and inference rules. A **study** asks a scientific question; an **experiment** instantiates a protocol/configuration; a **result** is an evidence-backed statement derived from that experiment or a proof. These terms are kept separate in `registry/catalog.json`.

## 4. Static attainable capability and organizational realization

For a fixed competence/state description \(S\), demand \(P\), constraints \(B\), and admissible policy class \(\Pi\), let \(\Pi_B\) be the policies feasible under \(B\). If policy \(\pi\) yields outcome vector \(z^\pi=(y^\pi,r^\pi)\), where larger effectiveness \(y\) and smaller resource use \(r\) are preferred, define
\[
\mathcal F_\Pi(S,P,B)=\{z^\pi(S,P):\pi\in\Pi_B\},\qquad
\mathcal C_\Pi=ND[\mathcal F_\Pi].
\]
Here \(ND\) retains Pareto-nondominated outcomes. The definitions distinguish feasible outcomes, efficient capability, realized performance, and utility/value. Scalar utility is not assumed. If randomization/time-sharing is admissible and outcome aggregation is linear, a convex closure may be appropriate; convexification is not an HLS axiom.

**Feasible capability:** the set above is a definition, not a theorem about any particular system.

**Collective capability expansion:** compare a richer policy class to an appropriate reproducibility/dominance closure of a baseline. A strict expansion exists only if the richer class has an outcome not dominated by any baseline outcome. Class inclusion alone gives weak attainability inclusion; realized value depends on demand and constraints.

**Organizational sufficiency:** a simpler organization is sufficient for a specified evaluation when the richer class adds no relevant efficient capability/value under the stated conditions. This is conditional, not universal.

**Organizational realization:** describes how an admissible organization/policy selects actions that realize outcomes in the feasible set. It is a mapping, not an additional performance guarantee.

**Policy-class inclusion proposition.** If \(\Pi_{SEP}\subseteq\Pi_{HLS}\), both classes are evaluated under the same state/world, feasible constraints, information, outcome functional and objective, then \(\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_{SEP}}J(\pi)\leq\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_{HLS}}J(\pi)\). This is the standard supremum-over-subset inequality. If both maxima are attained, equality holds exactly when at least one HLS-maximizing policy belongs to \(\Pi_{SEP}\): one direction follows because such a policy attains the HLS maximum in SEP; in the other direction, an SEP maximizer attaining the common maximum is also HLS-optimal. Without the common evaluation contract or attainment, this iff statement does not follow as written. This result says nothing about how often strict inequality occurs or whether a particular organization can implement the larger class.

### Exact finite static allocation results

The retained two-agent/two-task cases are exact finite constructions. For crossed specialization with task success/effectiveness matrix \((y_{11},y_{12};y_{21},y_{22})\), and \(p=P(q_1)\), the best one-agent policy is compared with task-wise routing. If strict crossed advantage holds, let \(A_1=|y_{11}-y_{21}|\), \(A_2=|y_{12}-y_{22}|\). Then:
\[
\Delta_Y(p)=\min(pA_1,(1-p)A_2),\quad p^*=\frac{A_2}{A_1+A_2},\quad \max_p\Delta_Y=\frac{A_1A_2}{A_1+A_2}.
\]
This follows by choosing the better same-agent endpoint versus the independently selected task-wise outcomes; the two linear branches meet at \(pA_1=(1-p)A_2\). It is restricted to this finite equal-resource scalar-effectiveness model. The symmetric instance (1,.2;.2,1) yields \(0.8\min(p,1-p)\), maximum 0.4 at p=.5; the asymmetric instance (.9,.5;.3,.9) yields maximum .24 at p=.4.

The resource-constrained extension uses points \(z_{ab}=(R_{ab},Y_{ab})\) and convex time-sharing hulls for ONE and ROUTE. On their common feasible budget domain, class inclusion implies \(Y^*_{ROUTE}\ge Y^*_{ONE}\). This is an exact optimization property under the declared convexified feasible sets, not a novel general theorem. In a finite control with p=.5, the positive-gain budget region is (.25,.65), maximum gain .0625 at B=.40; another control has geometric area but zero efficient gain. Thus set expansion/area does not by itself imply value.

## 5. Action-dependent state change and future development value

The minimal endogenous dynamic boundary is an action-dependent state transition:
\[
S_{t+1}=F(S_t,a_t).
\]
For future conditions \(\theta_{t+1}=(P_{t+1},B_{t+1},\Pi_{t+1})\), compare future efficient capabilities \(\mathcal C_{t+1}(a)\) and a model-specific future value \(V_{t+1}(F(S_t,a);\theta_{t+1})\).

- **State-to-capability boundary:** actions can lead to different future capability sets, but state/competence change need not change the efficient collective frontier.
- **Capability-to-value boundary:** a capability difference may be irrelevant to a given future demand/objective/horizon; capability change does not imply positive development value.
- **Current/future value decomposition:** in the minimum two-period comparison, \(\Delta J=\Delta R+\Delta^{dev}\), where \(\Delta R\) is current operational value difference and \(\Delta^{dev}\) the future value difference. This is a standard continuation-value decomposition, not a novel universal law.

Under common future conditions and value evaluated from the same capability object, equality of future capability implies equality of future value. This implication depends on those assumptions. The relevance chain in the source theory is a structural screen, not a new dynamic-programming theorem.

### Exact finite dynamic constructions

The retained deterministic two-agent × two-task × two-period model with a specified learning-by-doing transition and terminal task routing. A no-learning control has equal future states. A second case changes a state from \(((.4,.8),(.8,.4))\) to \(((.5,.8),(.8,.4))\) under one action, while both efficient terminal frontiers remain \{(.8,.8)\}; state change does not entail collective frontier or value change. In a value-reversal case, \(\Delta R=-.1\), \(\Delta^{dev}=.2p\), and \(\Delta J=-.1+.2p\); at p=.75 the development term reverses the immediate preference. These are exact results only for the declared model, not evidence that learning or joint management generally helps.

## 7. Relationship to established theory

The finite allocation comparisons are instances of constrained assignment and Pareto analysis; their class-inclusion inequalities follow from feasible-set inclusion, not a new general optimization principle. The dynamic recursion and continuation terms are standard dynamic-programming objects [@puterman1994mdp; @bertsekas2017dynamic]. The value of acquiring decision-relevant information connects to value-of-information theory and comparison of experiments [@howard1966information; @blackwell1951comparison; @blackwell1953equivalent]. Queueing and deadline results require queue-specific assumptions; the classical long-run queue identity is not a deadline-success theorem [@little1961proof]. Task allocation with endogenous training and organizational learning is also established prior work [@baccara2023task; @nembhard2015selection]. These connections interpret the retained constructions and do not extend their scope.

The active exact implementations are in src/hls/static_assignment.py and src/hls/dynamic_development.py, with small study runners and contract tests. Historical source commit c81def3b5280b0f59d6dcbfd3e25df8805f5013c contains derivations at the paths recorded in docs/RESEARCH.md and registry/catalog.json. Only results whose arguments are reproduced here are presented as independently checked in this branch; remaining source propositions are historical and unverified here.

## 8. Open theoretical questions

The retained source identifies, but does not solve generally: sufficient information for collective decisions under changing organizational constraints; conditions for a strict root-level joint/separated value gap; conditions under which learning changes an efficient frontier rather than only an individual state; predictive value of competence development across environments; causal pathways from organization to collective performance; and transferability of finite results across task/demand structures. These are open questions, not established HLS laws.

## Position of the programme

The motivating question remains:

> “How should a heterogeneous system dynamically allocate tasks among models/agents while simultaneously deciding how to redistribute the knowledge generated by those executions so that the system deliberately evolves its division of competences?”

RQ0 remains the sole official repository research question and is unchanged. H1 remains a working hypothesis, not an official RQ:

> “Failure frequency and quality gap are insufficient statistics for selecting competence-improvement actions in a heterogeneous model portfolio. A useful selection policy should account for the expected learnability of the recipient model and the downstream operational value created by the competence change.”

The consolidated question behind H1 is not only whether learnability augments `frequency * quality_gap`. It is how a competence-changing action should be valued through its expected effect on future system operation.

## Methodological discipline

Papers are mined for mechanisms, assumptions, limitations, and evidence—not for matching keywords. Overlap is not treated as disqualifying, and papers are not treated as threats merely because they share components with HLS. Mathematical equivalence or full coverage of the decision problem would matter; superficial resemblance would not. No novelty claim follows from this audit.

## Domains audited and principal lessons

- **Routing / RouteNLP.** RouteNLP provides the concrete empirical adversary: it clusters routing escalations, applies targeted frontier-to-cheaper-tier distillation, and retrains the router. It demonstrates a closed operational loop and cost-oriented evidence. Its selection heuristic is approximately cluster size/frequency times mean quality gap; the recipient hierarchy and objective are more restricted than a general portfolio intervention decision.
- **Machine Teaching / curriculum.** Iterative, black-box, state-dependent, heterogeneous, and cost-aware teaching mechanisms already exist. The corrected conclusion is not that targets are always fixed, but that the audited formulations primarily specify a learner/teaching objective rather than the future operational value of modifying one member of a model portfolio.
- **Active Learning / Value of Information.** Expected error reduction and EPIG value future prediction; decision-aware active learning and VoI value information by later decisions. Information gain, prediction accuracy, decision accuracy, and operational utility are distinct. In classical VoI an acquisition changes beliefs; an HLS intervention can change the operational system itself.
- **Bandits / resource allocation.** Contextual, restless, rising, graph-triggered, budgeted, and delayed-feedback formulations provide mechanisms for uncertain, repeated intervention allocation. They support the possibility of learning recipient-specific response and transition structure, but a learning gain is not automatically a portfolio-level reward.
- **Capacity expansion / optimal control / real options.** The useful analogy is competence as capacity, training as investment, routing as dispatch, and task demand as uncertain demand. It foregrounds timing, irreversibility, waiting, lead time, depreciation, flexibility, and option value. It does not imply that uncertainty monotonically increases diversity.
- **Continual / lifelong learning.** A training action may induce forward transfer, backward transfer, forgetting, negative transfer, and interference. Thus competence change cannot generally be represented as a scalar positive increment. Individual forgetting need not be system-level harm if the portfolio retains cheaper or better coverage.
- **Decision-focused / bilevel optimization.** Transition prediction error, intervention-selection regret, and final operational utility must remain distinct. A transition representation need only be adequate for good intervention decisions; however, decision-focused optimization cannot repair a misspecified downstream objective and may require robustness against model exploitation.

Quality-Diversity remains transversal: diversity can be a consequence relevant to future utility, not an assumed bonus such as `J + lambda * Diversity`.

## Current minimal ontology

```text
C_t                         competence landscape / system capability state
x_t ~ D_t                   operational task demand
a_t                         routing / execution action
u_t                         learning / competence-modification intervention, including null
C_{t+1} ~ P(C' | C_t, u_t)  stochastic competence evolution
```

The conceptual chain is:

```text
routing -> operational experience -> learning intervention / knowledge transfer
    -> changed competence landscape -> future routing
```

The physical transition `P*(C' | C,u)` must be kept distinct from a learned approximation `P_hat_phi` or `T_hat_phi`, and both must be kept distinct from the regret of the intervention policy they induce. The eventual value must derive from future operation, not competence gain alone. This ontology is scaffolding; it does not fix a Bellman equation, state observability, or whether the eventual formalism is a bandit, MDP, POMDP, bilevel problem, or real-options model.

## Claims explicitly not made

The programme does not establish novelty of H1; optimality or multiplicativity of the earlier conceptual decomposition; a sufficient observable `C_t`; an estimator of learnability or downstream value; that diversity belongs explicitly in an objective; that uncertainty implies more diversity; that RouteNLP is suboptimal; or that a practical policy can outperform it after all costs are counted.

## Unresolved questions

How should competence be observed or represented? Which effects of an intervention are material to future routing? How should positive transfer and interference be estimated? What comparison class makes H1 falsifiable? When does a restricted intervention policy beat `frequency * quality_gap` after data-generation, training, estimation, and operational costs? These questions remain open.

## Closure decision and next phase

Broad horizontal searching is stopped provisionally. The audit is sufficient to prevent treating routing, teaching, information acquisition, allocation, capacity investment, continual learning, or decision-focused learning as absent components. It is not a completeness or novelty proof.

The next phase is a minimal model and a synthetic falsification experiment. Neither is designed or started by this checkpoint. The purpose of that next phase is to make H1 vulnerable to early negative evidence.

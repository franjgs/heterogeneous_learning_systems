# Minimal HLS Mathematical Model

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the minimal mathematical model used to test the central
HLS hypothesis:

> Can joint management of operational allocation and competence development
> improve long-term system performance compared with architectures that manage
> these decisions separately?

The purpose of this model is not to establish novelty. It is to determine
whether the proposed joint management can provide a genuine performance
advantage under explicit conditions.

The model is deliberately minimal and falsifiable. Additional mechanisms should
not be introduced merely to obtain an advantage for joint management.

---

## 2. Minimal system

Consider two agents

\[
\mathcal M=\{1,2\},
\]

one competence, and a two-period horizon

\[
t\in\{1,2\}.
\]

The competence of agent \(m\) at the beginning of period \(t\) is

\[
S_{m,t}\ge 0.
\]

Each period has an exogenous workload

\[
D_t\ge 0,
\]

and agent \(m\) has capacity

\[
b_m>0.
\]

---

## 3. Decisions

Operational allocation:

\[
a_{m,t}\ge 0,
\]

where \(a_{m,t}\) is the amount of operational work performed by agent \(m\).

Competence-development allocation:

\[
d_{m,t}\ge 0,
\]

where \(d_{m,t}\) is capacity devoted to competence development rather than
operational work.

The decision is therefore

\[
u_t=(\mathbf a_t,\mathbf d_t).
\]

Feasibility requires

\[
a_{m,t}+d_{m,t}\le b_m
\]

and

\[
\sum_m a_{m,t}\le D_t.
\]

Operational work and competence development therefore compete for the same
finite capacity.

---

## 4. Operational value

Work performed is

\[
W_{m,t}=a_{m,t}.
\]

For the minimal realization, expected quality is represented by current
competence,

\[
\bar Q_{m,t}=S_{m,t},
\]

and operational value is

\[
Y_t=\sum_m S_{m,t}a_{m,t}.
\]

This is a particular linear realization of the HLS ontology; it does not
identify competence and operational quality in general.

---

## 5. Experience and learning

Direct experience is generated by operational work:

\[
E^{dir}_{m,t}=a_{m,t}.
\]

Indirect experience is generated by competence-development activity:

\[
E^{ind}_{m,t}=d_{m,t}.
\]

Competence evolves according to

\[
S_{m,t+1}
=
S_{m,t}
+\eta_m a_{m,t}
+\lambda_m d_{m,t},
\]

where

\[
\eta_m\ge0
\]

is the learning rate from operational experience and

\[
\lambda_m\ge0
\]

is the learning rate from explicit competence development.

No saturation, forgetting, interference, noise, or cross-agent transfer is
introduced in the minimal model.

---

## 6. Objective

The system objective is

\[
J(\pi)=\mathbb E_\pi[Y_1+Y_2].
\]

No terminal value is assigned directly to competence. Consequently,

\[
d_{m,2}^*=0.
\]

The essential HLS feedback loop is therefore

\[
(\mathbf a_1,\mathbf d_1)
\rightarrow
(Y_1,\mathbf S_2)
\rightarrow
Y_2.
\]

Both operational allocation and competence development affect future
competence, while both compete for current capacity.

---

## 7. Joint management

Joint management chooses operational allocation and competence development
simultaneously:

\[
(\mathbf a^J,\mathbf d^J)
\in
\arg\max_{\mathbf a,\mathbf d}J(\mathbf a,\mathbf d)
\]

subject to the physical constraints and true competence dynamics.

Thus the joint controller internalizes simultaneously

\[
a\rightarrow Y,
\qquad
a\rightarrow S'\rightarrow Y',
\]

and

\[
d\rightarrow \text{available operational capacity},
\qquad
d\rightarrow S'\rightarrow Y'.
\]

---

## 8. Sequential separated management

Separated management uses the same physical system, parameters, information,
horizon, learning laws, and final performance criterion as joint management.

The difference is architectural: operational allocation and competence
development are optimized sequentially rather than jointly.

Two priority orders are considered.

### 8.1 Operation first: A -> D

The operational subsystem first solves

\[
\mathbf a^S
\in
\arg\max_{\mathbf a}J(\mathbf a,\mathbf 0).
\]

It is not myopic: it accounts for future learning generated by operational
experience.

The development subsystem then solves

\[
\mathbf d^S
\in
\arg\max_{\mathbf d}J(\mathbf a^S,\mathbf d)
\]

subject to the residual capacity

\[
d_{m,t}\le b_m-a_{m,t}^S.
\]

### 8.2 Development first: D -> A

The development subsystem first determines competence-development allocation
without operational allocation.

The operational subsystem subsequently uses the remaining capacity.

This provides the reverse fixed-priority separated architecture.

---

## 9. Closed-form T=2 result under full workload

Consider

\[
b_1=b_2=1
\]

and sufficient workload to use all available operational capacity.

Let period 2 contribute with probability \(p\).

For each agent define

\[
V_m^A=S_{m,1}+p\eta_m
\]

as the total value of one unit of capacity devoted to operational work in
period 1, including its future learning effect, and

\[
V_m^D=p\lambda_m
\]

as the future value of one unit devoted to competence development.

Joint management chooses

\[
(a_m^J,d_m^J)=
\begin{cases}
(1,0), & V_m^A>V_m^D,\\
(0,1), & V_m^D>V_m^A,
\end{cases}
\]

with any full-capacity mixture optimal at equality.

---

## 10. Operation-first separated architecture

The operational subsystem occupies the available capacity because

\[
V_m^A>0.
\]

Hence

\[
a_{m,1}^{A\rightarrow D}=1,
\qquad
d_{m,1}^{A\rightarrow D}=0.
\]

The performance gap is

\[
\boxed{
J_J-J_{A\rightarrow D}
=
\sum_m [V_m^D-V_m^A]_+
}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
J_J>J_{A\rightarrow D}
\iff
\exists m:\;V_m^D>V_m^A.
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
p\lambda_m>S_{m,1}+p\eta_m.
\]

---

## 11. Development-first separated architecture

The development subsystem occupies the available capacity first, giving

\[
d_{m,1}^{D\rightarrow A}=1,
\qquad
a_{m,1}^{D\rightarrow A}=0.
\]

The corresponding performance gap is

\[
\boxed{
J_J-J_{D\rightarrow A}
=
\sum_m [V_m^A-V_m^D]_+
}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
J_J>J_{D\rightarrow A}
\iff
\exists m:\;V_m^A>V_m^D.
}
\]

---

## 12. First analytical result

The two fixed-priority separated architectures fail in complementary regions:

\[
A\rightarrow D
\quad\text{is suboptimal when}\quad
V_m^D>V_m^A,
\]

whereas

\[
D\rightarrow A
\quad\text{is suboptimal when}\quad
V_m^A>V_m^D.
\]

Thus no fixed priority between operational allocation and competence
development is universally optimal in the minimal model.

Joint management selects the higher-value use of capacity according to the
system state and parameters.

This establishes an initial existence result: there are explicit, non-empty
parameter regions in which joint management strictly outperforms each of the
two fixed-priority separated architectures.

---

## 13. Scope of the result

This result does NOT establish that joint management outperforms every possible
architecture that could be described as separated.

In particular, a separated architecture with sufficiently rich coordination
or iterative information exchange may reproduce the joint solution.

The current result establishes something narrower:

> For a well-defined class of sequential separated architectures with fixed
> priority, joint management has explicit regions of strict performance
> advantage.

This is retained as the first theoretical support for the HLS architecture.

Future work should test the robustness of this result against stronger
separated baselines without adding mechanisms merely to force a joint
advantage.

---

## 14. Status

**Minimal HLS model v1: CONSOLIDATED.**

Established:

1. minimal physical model;
2. common capacity constraint for operation and development;
3. direct and indirect learning mechanisms;
4. finite-horizon objective without artificial terminal competence reward;
5. joint policy;
6. operation-first separated policy;
7. development-first separated policy;
8. exact T=2 performance gaps;
9. explicit equality and strict-advantage regions.

Next theoretical task:

> Determine how far the strict-advantage result survives against stronger but
> genuinely separated architectures.

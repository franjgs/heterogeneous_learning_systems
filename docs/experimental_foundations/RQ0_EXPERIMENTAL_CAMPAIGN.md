## 2. RQ0

Use the canonical RQ0 defined in docs/research_questions.md.

The experimental interpretation of RQ0 is:

Under what world structures and conditions does jointly managing task allocation and competence development produce long-term system value that cannot be reproduced by a fair separated architecture?

The objective is not to prove that HLS always outperforms SEP.

The objective is to identify:

* regions where integration has value;
* regions where integration has no value;
* reducibility boundaries;
* mechanisms responsible for those regions;
* conditions under which an apparent advantage disappears.

The desired scientific product is a phase map of integration value, not a collection of favorable examples.

⸻

## 4. Available experimental capabilities

The first campaign may use the following implemented capabilities.

C1 — Repeated endogenous competence evolution

Current actions can modify competence, which can modify later decisions and subsequent competence evolution.

This permits genuine recursive routing–learning feedback and path dependence.

C2 — Collective portfolio geometry

The framework supports genuine (M,K>2) competence structures and can represent:

* specialization;
* complementarity;
* redundancy;
* dominance and non-dominance;
* collective coverage;
* competence gaps.

C3 — Scarce competence-development resources

Development resources can be persistent, scarce and exhaustible.

Development alternatives can therefore compete for a genuinely limited resource.

C4 — Competence interactions

Development can induce signed interactions between competence coordinates through (\Gamma):

[
\Gamma=0
]

for independence,

[
\Gamma>0
]

for transfer, and

[
\Gamma<0
]

for interference.

This establishes the basic C4 interaction capability. It does not imply that every possible C4 mechanism such as forgetting, finite capacity or heterogeneous learning rates has been implemented.

C5 — Dynamic task demand

The framework supports:

* deterministic finite demand;
* stationary stochastic demand;
* non-stationary demand;
* first-order Markov demand.

This provides the demand-side variation required for the first RQ0 campaign.

## 7. Campaign structure

The first RQ0 campaign has four stages.

They are designed to progress from causal identification to phase mapping and then demand robustness.

RQ0-A — Reducibility and negative controls

Purpose:

Establish worlds in which integration should have no structural value.

Candidate controls include:

* no effective competence development;
* no routing-dependent opportunity generation;
* non-binding development resources;
* redundant competence portfolios;
* independent competence development;
* simple or degenerate task demand;
* absence of genuine recursive feedback.

Expected behavior in valid reducible controls is typically

[
J_{\mathrm{HLS}}=J_{\mathrm{SEP}}.
]

A systematic HLS advantage in worlds constructed to be separable is a warning that the comparison, solver or world construction may be biased.

A1 and the C1 reference worlds provide anchors, but RQ0-A is a campaign-level reducibility test rather than a repetition of A1.

⸻

RQ0-B — Minimal mechanism of non-separability

Purpose:

Determine which combination of mechanisms is sufficient to produce genuine integration value.

Start from reducible controls and activate mechanisms systematically.

Principal causal dimensions are:

* C1: repeated endogenous feedback;
* C2: portfolio geometry;
* C3: scarce development allocation;
* C4: competence interactions;
* opportunity coupling inherited from A1.

Use controlled ablations rather than a blind Cartesian sweep.

The scientific question is:

Which minimal combination of mechanisms breaks separability?

Possible outcomes include, for example, that recursive feedback alone is insufficient but feedback combined with specialization and scarcity creates a non-separable region.

No such outcome is assumed prospectively.

RQ0-B must include configurations where integration does not help.

⸻

RQ0-C — Phase maps and boundaries

Purpose:

Once a minimal non-separable mechanism has been identified, map the region in which its effect exists and locate the boundaries where it disappears.

Only after RQ0-B should dense or structured parameter sweeps be used.

Candidate axes include:

Portfolio geometry

Vary:

* specialization;
* redundancy;
* complementarity;
* dominance;
* competence gaps;
* (M);
* (K).

Development dynamics

Vary development strength such as

[
\eta
]

and competence interaction

[
\Gamma.
]

Include explicitly:

[
\Gamma<0,\qquad
\Gamma=0,\qquad
\Gamma>0.
]

Scarcity

Move from:

[
\text{non-binding}
\rightarrow
\text{moderately scarce}
\rightarrow
\text{strongly scarce}.
]

Opportunity coupling

Vary the degree to which operational execution affects development opportunity generation, including the A1 coupling parameter (\rho) where applicable.

The desired output is a set of interpretable phase diagrams showing where

[
\Delta J_{\mathrm{cons}}
]

appears, grows, vanishes or changes sign.

⸻

RQ0-D — Demand dynamics

Purpose:

Determine whether the phase structure identified in RQ0-B/C depends on the temporal structure of task demand.

Do not initially repeat the entire RQ0-C grid.

Select representative configurations from:

* (\mathcal R_0);
* the phase boundary;
* (\mathcal R_+);
* any discovered (\mathcal R_-).

Then vary C5 demand.

Stationary demand

[
Q_t\sim p(q).
]

Vary task concentration and balance.

Non-stationary demand

Use regime changes such as

[
p_1(q)\rightarrow p_2(q).
]

Study whether competence investment made before a demand shift changes integration value.

Markov demand

Vary temporal persistence through

[
P(Q_{t+1}=q\mid Q_t=q).
]

The central question is:

Does the region of non-separability persist, disappear or move when the temporal structure of demand changes?

## 9. Prospective interpretation rules

These rules must be fixed before examining campaign results.

Evidence consistent with meaningful integration value

RQ0 receives experimental support if a non-degenerate region exists in which

[
\Delta J_{\mathrm{cons}}>0,
]

and the effect:

* persists under reasonable parameter perturbations;
* is not produced by asymmetric information;
* is not produced by an artificially weak SEP;
* can be related to identifiable mechanisms;
* disappears or changes predictably when those mechanisms are removed.

Strong limitation of RQ0

RQ0 is strongly limited if positive integration value occurs only:

* at isolated points;
* under extreme parameter settings;
* under fine tuning;
* because SEP is artificially restricted;
* because HLS receives privileged information;
* or because the world construction itself embeds the desired result.

Scientifically meaningful negative result

If

[
\Delta J_{\mathrm{cons}}\approx0
]

throughout a broad and scientifically relevant region, this is not an experimental failure.

It may demonstrate that a sufficiently strong separated architecture reproduces the integrated solution over a much larger domain than expected.

That is a reducibility result.

SEP-(\Omega)

If

[
J_{\mathrm{HLS}}

J_{\mathrm{SEP-\Omega}}
]

systematically, determine what coordination or continuation information SEP-(\Omega) requires.

Do not relabel this equality as HLS superiority.

Such a result may motivate C6 later, but C6 must not be implemented prospectively merely because it exists on the capability map.

The initial campaign uses

\[

\[

### 14.3 C3 scarcity

For the two-cycle initial campaign, let

\[

\]

denote the maximum number of effective development actions available over the

Interpretation:

- `B=0`: NO-DEVELOPMENT control;

The central RQ0-B world uses

\[

### 14.4 C4 competence interaction

The preregistered interaction values are

\[

They represent:

- `gamma=-0.25`: moderate interference;
- `gamma=0`: independent competence development;
- `gamma=+0.25`: moderate positive transfer.

campaign must not redefine C4 in response to results.

The central RQ0-B world uses

\[
\gamma=0.

- `rho=0`: opportunity generation is decoupled from the executor;

The central RQ0-B world uses

\[

### 14.13 Frozen C3 budget unit and C4 interaction topology

Before implementation, two remaining physical semantics are fixed
prospectively.

#### C3 budget unit

For the RQ0-A/B campaign,

\[
\texttt{cost\_per\_development}=1.
\]

Therefore the preregistered initial budgets

\[
B\in\{0,1,2\}
\]

correspond exactly to a maximum of zero, one, or two effective non-null
development actions over the two-cycle horizon.

Null development has zero cost.

This convention connects the preregistered discrete interpretation of \(B\)
directly to the already implemented C3 continuous budget semantics; it does
not introduce a new resource mechanism.

#### C4 interaction topology

For a non-null development action targeting competence \(k\) of learner
\(M_i\), C4 interactions are restricted to the other competence coordinates
of that same learner.

Formally,

\[
\Gamma_{(M_i,k)\rightarrow(M_j,\ell)}
=
\begin{cases}
\gamma, & i=j,\ \ell\neq k,\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Thus direct development updates the selected coordinate \((M_i,k)\) according
to the existing C4 direct-development rule, while the two non-target
competences of \(M_i\) receive the signed interaction already defined by
`CoupledDevelopmentKernel`.

No C4 interaction directly modifies another learner.

For \(K=3\), for example, development of \((M_1,2)\) directly develops
\(c_{1,2}\) and applies coefficient \(\gamma\) to \(c_{1,1}\) and \(c_{1,3}\).
All coordinates of \(M_2\) and \(M_3\) receive zero C4 interaction from that
action.

The preregistered values remain

\[
\gamma\in\{-0.25,0,+0.25\}.
\]

Consequently:

- `gamma=0` exactly recovers independent competence development;
- `gamma=+0.25` represents moderate intra-learner positive transfer;
- `gamma=-0.25` represents moderate intra-learner interference.

This topology is fixed before campaign execution and must not be changed in
response to observed HLS/SEP performance.

It deliberately does not introduce direct inter-learner transfer through C4.
Any future inter-learner knowledge-transfer mechanism would constitute a
separate experimental extension and is outside this RQ0-A/B protocol.

### 14.14 Frozen development and opportunity semantics

RQ0-A/B worlds are fixed here before any campaign configuration is evaluated.

\[
\eta=0.75.
\]

This value is inherited from the validated C1 reference construction. Variation
of \(\eta\) remains reserved for RQ0-C and is not part of the initial RQ0-A/B
campaign.

Development targets follow the future task sequence:

\[
t=0\rightarrow k=2,\qquad
t=1\rightarrow k=3.
\]

Thus each development opportunity may improve the competence associated with
the next operational or terminal task. The target schedule is identical for
all nine configurations.

Opportunity generation retains the existing A1 mixture semantics

\[
g_i(q)=(1-\rho)\bar e_q+\rho e_{i,q}.
\]

For the two operational tasks,

\[
\bar e_1=\bar e_2=0.5.
\]

The executor-specific values are frozen from the initial competence geometry:

\[
e_{i,q}=c_{i,q}^{(0)},\qquad q\in\{1,2\}.
\]

They are parameters of the world instantiated from \(C(s)\); they are not
updated when competence evolves during a trajectory.

Consequently, at \(\rho=0\),

\[
g_i(q)=0.5
\]

for every executor, providing exact removal of executor-dependent opportunity
generation.

At \(s=0\), all executor-specific values are equal within each operational
task, so opportunity generation is also independent of executor identity even
when \(\rho>0\).

For specialized portfolios, executor dependence arises from the preregistered
C2 competence geometry itself rather than from manually privileged learner
labels.

These semantics are fixed prospectively and must not be modified in response
to RQ0-A/B results.

### 15.4 Sufficient local reducibility condition

The exact recursion provides a simple explanation for the observed result.

Suppose that, at a non-terminal state \(S\), routing action \(a\) affects
future value only through the probability \(p_a\) of generating a development
opportunity, and that conditional on opportunity realization the admissible
development decisions, costs, resource semantics, and state transitions are
independent of the identity of the executor.

Let

\[
V_0(S)
\]

be the optimal continuation value conditional on no opportunity and

\[
V_1(S)
\]

the optimal continuation value conditional on an available opportunity.

Then

\[
Q_{\mathrm{HLS}}(S,a)
=
R(S,q,a)
+
V_0(S)
+
p_a\left[V_1(S)-V_0(S)\right].
\]

Because null development remains admissible when an opportunity is available,

\[
V_1(S)\ge V_0(S).
\]

Therefore, if reward and opportunity probability satisfy the weak alignment
condition

\[
R(S,q,a)>R(S,q,b)
\Longrightarrow
p_a\ge p_b,
\]

then

\[
Q_{\mathrm{HLS}}(S,a)>Q_{\mathrm{HLS}}(S,b).
\]

It follows that

\[
\boxed{
\arg\max_a Q_{\mathrm{HLS}}(S,a)
\subseteq
\arg\max_a R(S,q,a).
}
\]

This is a sufficient local reducibility condition, not a necessary condition
and not a theorem about arbitrary HLS worlds.

The computational audit shows that this sufficient condition is respected
throughout all HLS-optimal reachable states in the nine preregistered
configurations.

### 15.5 Why the condition is not globally guaranteed by the world family

The opportunity model used in this campaign is

\[
p_i
=
(1-\rho)\bar e_q+\rho e_{i,q},
\]

where the executor values \(e_{i,q}\) are frozen from initial competence,
whereas operational reward is evaluated from current competence:

\[
R_i=c^{(t)}_{i,q}.
\]

Consequently, reward--opportunity alignment is not guaranteed for every
mathematically reachable state after endogenous competence evolution.

Development can in principle change the current competence ordering while
leaving the frozen executor-value ordering unchanged. A state may therefore
exist in which

\[
R_a>R_b
\]

but

\[
p_a<p_b.
\]

The current result must therefore not be interpreted as saying that the
campaign world representation is incapable of expressing a routing--future
conflict.

Rather, the observed result is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{all optimal HLS trajectories in the preregistered campaign remain
inside the audited reward--opportunity aligned region.}
}
\]

### 15.6 Boundary required for routing non-separability

For two routing actions \(a\) and \(b\),

\[
Q_a-Q_b
=
(R_a-R_b)
+
(p_a-p_b)\Delta V,
\]

where

\[
\Delta V=V_1-V_0\ge0.
\]

Suppose \(a\) is immediately preferable,

\[
R_a>R_b.
\]

For HLS to prefer the non-greedy action \(b\), reward--opportunity alignment
must first reverse,

\[
p_b>p_a,
\]

and the future value of the additional opportunity probability must be large
enough to compensate the immediate reward loss:

\[
(p_b-p_a)\Delta V>R_a-R_b.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\Delta V>
\frac{R_a-R_b}{p_b-p_a}
}
\qquad\text{when }p_b>p_a.
\]

Thus breaking reward--opportunity alignment is necessary for a routing
inversion under these executor-independent continuation semantics, but is not
sufficient.

This equation identifies an analytical boundary to investigate rather than a
parameter-search objective.

### 15.7 Interpretation and next research question

The first RQ0-B campaign has produced a negative integration-value result with
a positive structural interpretation.

The result is not:

> G0+C1--C5 is reducible.

Nor is it:

> HLS has no value.

The supported conclusion is narrower:

> In all nine preregistered RQ0-A/B configurations, exact HLS and strong SEP
> have equal value. Every exact HLS-optimal reachable non-terminal state
> preserves weak reward--opportunity alignment, and every HLS-optimal routing
> action is also immediately reward-optimal.

Accordingly, the initial campaign is closed as **B-REDUCIBLE**.

The anti-confirmation rule remains active: no new world or parameter sweep may
be introduced merely to obtain positive HLS advantage.

The next scientific question is instead:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Can endogenous competence evolution cross the routing reducibility
boundary under a non-artificial mechanism?}
}
\]

More specifically:

1. can endogenous evolution produce an optimal reachable state with
   \(p_b>p_a\) while \(R_a>R_b\);
2. can the resulting opportunity-value term become large enough to satisfy
   \[
   (p_b-p_a)\Delta V>R_a-R_b;
   \]
3. if so, does this produce a non-degenerate region with
   \[
   J_{\mathrm{HLS}}>J_{\mathrm{SEP,max}},
   \]
   rather than an isolated constructed counterexample?

No such result is assumed.

RQ0 therefore remains **OPEN**.

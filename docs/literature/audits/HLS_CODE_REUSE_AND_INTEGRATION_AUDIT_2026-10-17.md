# HLS Laboratory — code reuse and integration audit

**Audit date:** 2026-10-17
**Repository revision inspected:** `57f4ebfe7c51e7762a6a1d0a2cef3e18011b5501` (`feature/hls-laboratory-v1`)
**Scope:** read-only review of laboratory code, tests, runners, scientific documentation and the four nominated research references. No simulator, algorithm, result, PDF, or campaign was changed or executed.
**Evidence labels:** **FACT** = directly verified in source/test/document; **INFERENCE** = technical conclusion from those facts; **PROPOSAL** = integration not implemented or validated.

## 1. Executive assessment

The repository contains a substantial N×K experimental path, not merely a conceptual design: configurable competence matrices, additive production, functional/CES evaluation, resource-limited discrete actions, MIS-v2, directed teaching, bounded competence estimation, P0/P1/P2, C5-derived exogenous demand, replayable traces, and several matched-run helpers. Focused tests for the current laboratory contracts passed in this audit (184 passed; see §10).

That infrastructure is **not** a universal model adapter and does not make the four cited papers interchangeable. The strongest existing overlap is with Nembhard–Bentefouet at the level of agent/task assignment and learning-related organizational questions, but its productivity-as-a-function-of-direct-and-team-experience law and its selection/grouping stages are absent. HLS MIS-v2 is a different competence transition. MixLLM has the closest decision shape to single-candidate routing, but its learned quality/cost predictors, router uncertainty update, token economics and queue penalty are absent. Baccara et al. require service queues and capacity/training externalities that HLS does not represent. System-1.x requires structured plans, subgoals, verifiers, fast/deep operators and search budgets, which an HLS K-vector problem and one-period allocation do not provide.

**Recommendation: REFRAME, then conditional GO in bounded stages.** Reframe any proposed work as model-specific reproduction followed, only after fidelity checks, by explicitly labelled HLS adaptation or common-protocol comparison. Do not declare any of the four article mechanisms already reproduced by HLS. The first integration should be a **fidelity-gated Nembhard mechanism adapter/design**, not MixLLM by default and not a blended universal model: it is closest to HLS's agent/task/experience ontology and can reuse the current organization, task, assignment, exogenous-demand and trace scaffolding. It must remain a separately named alternative productivity state/transition until a unit- and outcome-preserving map to HLS competence/CES is demonstrated. If the missing original details identified in the mathematical extraction cannot be resolved, stop at a native/reference model and do not label the HLS adaptation as a reproduction.

No cross-paper numerical comparison is presently justified. Comparisons among HLS's own P0/P1/P2, MIS switches, or N×K organizations are possible in the supported N×K runner, but they answer HLS-model questions, not the original papers' claims.

### 2.2 Interpretation of “validated”

In the implementation inventory, **VALIDATED** means the corresponding code contract has focused tests, including historical 3×2 equivalence where named. It does not mean empirical calibration against organizations, external construct validation, replication of a cited paper, or proof that the scientific assumptions are realistic. “PARTIAL” means an adjacent or narrower capability exists, but not the nominated full mechanism. “EXPERIMENTAL” means an interface/prototype exists in a constrained synthetic subsystem, not as a supported general HLS Laboratory extension point. “NO DISPONIBLE” means no corresponding implementation was found in the audited repository paths; it is not a claim that the concept is scientifically impossible.

### 3.1 Exact current policy contract (not just labels)

The legacy 3×2 authority is `src/hls/hls_lab_v1_integrated.py`: `IntegratedPolicy`, `IntegratedDecisionView`, `_best_production`, `_best_action`, `P0ProductionOnly`, `P1FixedTeaching`, and `P2AdaptiveTeaching`. The N×K version is in `hls_lab_nk_policies.py` and its tests pin 508-action ordering and 3×2 decisions.

* **P0:** chooses among production-only candidates by the lexicographic result tuple `(functional success, -normalized deficit, margin, -effort)` and retains the first catalogued action on a tie. It does not choose teaching and does not maximize CES.
* **P1:** attempts its configured directed teacher–learner–skill relation, choosing the best production allocation conditional on that relation being feasible; otherwise falls back to the best production-only action. It is not a generic periodic training rule.
* **P2:** first selects best production if that action functionally succeeds. If no production-only candidate succeeds, it considers configured eligible relations (positive task requirement and learner estimate below target); chooses the first unseen relation, else the relation with the highest stored mean efficacy, teaching only if that mean meets the threshold. It uses observed formative efficacy memory and deterministic index tie-breaking. It does not calculate expected future organizational returns.

All three use the estimate in the policy view where imperfect mode is selected. The physical production transition uses actual competence. N×K P2's formative efficacy memory is separate from the competence posterior. P0 is always production-only; P1/P2 with teaching disabled still execute their legacy choice rule on the remaining production-only set/fallback, not a new algorithm.

## 5. Mapping to the HLS ontology

Use `docs/hls_ontology.md` as semantic guardrail. The mapping below is conceptual, not an equality claim.

| Article concept | HLS ontology object | Mapping status / condition |
|---|---|---|
| Nembhard worker, task type, assignment `x_ijt` | Agent `m`, task `q_t`, organization-use decision `a_t` | **RELATED**. Worker/task indices map as types; feasible sets differ (selection/grouping/exclusivity vs HLS effort allocation). |
| Nembhard productivity `P_ijt`, asymptote `k_ij`, experience `D,T` | Expected operational performance `Qbar`, exposure `E`, capability parameters | **RELATED**, not equivalent to normalized competence `S`. A task-duration and units map is prerequisite. |
| Baccara junior/senior assignment `q`, capacity `mu=f(x)`, waiting `W` | `a_t`, capacity `b`, latency `L(q)`, resource/cost `R,C` | **RELATED** only after queue and capacity representations; `mu` is not individual `S_mk`, and `W` is not HLS resource effort. |
| MixLLM query/model, predicted quality `p_hat`, price `c_hat`, uncertainty, chosen model | Problem `q_t`, expected performance `Qbar`, cost `C`, information state `Theta`, decision `a_t` | Selection type is conditionally **EQUIVALENT** (choose one candidate); predictions/cost/uncertainty are **RELATED** and require distinct calibrated variables. |
| System-1.x task state, subgoal, difficulty, plan segment, search states | Problem `q`, context `X_t`, sequence/coordination `a_t`, computational resource `R` | **RELATED** at the broad problem/decision level only. HLS K requirements are not plan segments; search states need their own resource unit. |

The ontology's distinctions must be preserved: competence ≠ performance; work ≠ experience ≠ resource; quality ≠ operational value; resource consumption ≠ capacity ≠ cost ≠ latency. Do not define one scalar common “utility” across throughput, CES, money, waiting time, tokens and search states absent a justified mapping.

## 6. Audit by investigation and defensible comparison levels

Levels used here: **1** faithful reproduction in original model/domain; **2** semantically justified HLS adaptation with losses explicit; **3** comparison under a common task/protocol/metric/constraints; **4** composition of mechanisms after independently validated adapters. A possible design is not an implemented experiment.

### 6.1 Nembhard–Bentefouet (2015)

**What the source describes.** The local primary-source extraction records direct hyperbolic learning/productivity, an indirect/team experience term, task-type-specific transfer, and worker selection→grouping→team assignment in parallel and serial structures. The original parameter fit/design and ambiguities (including main-text Eq. 3 vs Appendix A-4 period-duration `L`, some tie rules and solver detail) are in `docs/literature/audits/Nembhard_Bentefouet_2015_Mathematical_Extraction.md`. This model is not MIS-v2: the current HLS state transition updates `c`, then production is additive `e*c`; Nembhard productivity is directly a function of cumulative direct and weighted indirect experience.

**Levels.** Level 1: not reproduced by HLS current code; the extraction is source transcription, not an executable reproduction. Level 2: plausible but substantial adapter—introduce separately named experience and productivity state while retaining HLS mechanisms unaltered. Level 3: possible only after selecting a common task/output benchmark and matching assignment/information/resource controls; the existing HLS feasible action catalogue is not the original three-stage policy domain. Level 4: possible only after separately validating direct/indirect experience and organization decisions; do not combine its indirect transfer with HLS explicit teaching as if equivalent.

**Defensible bridge question.** Conditional on matched initial productivity and fixed assignments/exposure, how do the two declared experience laws respond to direct exposure and to peer/task-type exposure, and how does that affect output on a declared common task sequence? This estimates an adaptation's model sensitivity, not that HLS reproduces the 2015 paper or confirms its empirical setting.

### 6.2 Baccara, Lee & Yariv (2023)

**What the source describes.** The registered article studies task assignment and on-the-job training in a service/queueing setting where allocation affects provider expertise/capacity and congestion/waiting, and contrasts centralized allocation with decentralized choice/equilibrium. The source register identifies the local file as an author manuscript dated 2022-11-24, 44 pages, not the final typeset journal version. The prior cross-model matrix records a senior capacity function driven by junior load and a queue waiting/stability condition; it also explicitly says that matrix was not a new complete extraction of this PDF. This audit therefore relies on that scoped prior formalization and does not claim a line-by-line replication of the article.

**Levels.** Level 1: absent. Level 2: no HLS-runner adaptation is currently semantically faithful because the queue/capacity mechanism is central, not peripheral. A separate analytical or queue model could preserve the article, but that would be a new model component and requires PI scope. Level 3: not presently defensible in the HLS episode runner. Level 4: defer; adding individual MIS/teaching to an aggregate stationary service-capacity queue would require a specified bridge and separate factorial controls.

**Not supported by current HLS:** congestion, waiting, arrivals during service, service completion, queue stability, endogenous senior service rate, social optimum versus equilibrium. A sequence with one problem each period does not imply a queue.

### 6.3 Wang et al. (2025), MixLLM

**What the source describes.** `HLS_ROUTING_AUDIT_2026-10-17.md` transcribes MixLLM's query/model-specific quality and token-cost predictors, quality-cost score, uncertainty bonus, waiting penalty, argmax model choice, and offline/online predictor updates (equations 1–20). It explicitly distinguishes router learning from learning of candidate LLM competence. The local PDF register validates the proceedings source. HLS has no equivalent quality/cost prediction, contextual query embedding, token price, per-model queue, or router-specific Bayesian/gradient update. HLS posterior estimates agent-skill competence; P2 memory estimates directed teaching efficacy; neither is a MixLLM router predictor.

**Levels.** Level 1: not reproduced. Level 2: the narrow `NKCoordinationPolicy.choose` seam can host a one-candidate selector after a query-feature and candidate-quality/cost interface exists, but a selector without source score, learning state, or queue term is only `MixLLM-inspired`. Level 3: a common routing comparison is plausible if all selectors receive identical candidate set, query features, feedback, quality labels, cost/latency measurements and training/test split. Existing HLS P0 is not a MixLLM baseline unless HLS decision objectives are made commensurate. Level 4: defer until predictor and router learning are independently tested and separated from expert competence transitions.

**Existing adjacent code, not equivalence.** `experiments/synthetic/campaign3_fast_deep_meta/run.py` and `campaign3_meta_voc/confirmatory.py` have conditional FAST/DEEP/META and META-VOC comparisons in a specific planning/reference model, with measured selector/solver compute. They do not route among heterogeneous LLMs or implement MixLLM quality-cost predictors.

### 6.4 Saha et al. (2025), System-1.x

**What the source describes.** The prior routing audit formalizes task decomposition into subgoals, difficulty estimate (h), a hybridization parameter and selection of a contiguous deep-search segment, with fast operators for remaining segments and training over plans/search traces. Reported measures include plan validity and explored states. This is intra-task sequential planning composition, unlike HLS assignment of current competence across agents/skills, and unlike MixLLM's choice of one model per query.

**Levels.** Level 1: not reproduced. Level 2: unavailable until a task domain has state transitions, goals, verified subgoals/plans, fast/deep operators, and search budget. Level 3: possible in a separate verifiable planning benchmark; no current HLS problem contract can support it. Level 4: defer until standalone fidelity and a justified conversion/parallel reporting of search states, latency and other resources. HLS must not treat competence dimensions as subgoals merely because both are vectors.

### 6.5 Cross-paper comparison limits

There is no common natural outcome or resource unit among manufacturing throughput, queue welfare/wait, LLM answer quality/token price, and verified planning/search states. A common-protocol comparison would therefore need a new shared task domain, common observable outcomes, action feasibility and resource vector, and information/training protocol. Without a theoretically justified scalar conversion, report outcome and resource dimensions separately. Do not use HLS CES as a universal translation.

## 8. Historical HLS evidence and novelty guard

`docs/HLS_HISTORICAL_MEMORY_AND_NOVELTY_GUARD.md` records the established boundaries:

* **G1:** static capability distributions, feasible organizations and demand; heterogeneity and equal aggregate capability need not imply equal achievable value.
* **G2/MIS:** dynamic assignment and individual MIS learning can create present sacrifice/future gain in a constructed setting; learning alone does not imply collective value.
* **G3:** development value depends on assignment sensitivity, substitutability, comparative advantage and feasible alternatives.
* **G3-H:** simple local summaries can mis-rank when a future joint opportunity matters; look-ahead/DP restores continuation in its frozen scope.
* **RQ0:** policy class restrictions and endogenous opportunities delimit strict joint-vs-strong-separable comparisons; no general collective superiority follows.
* **Campaign 1/2:** document pilot, mechanism interaction, teaching counterfactual and adaptive efficacy observations, with causal/sample limits. Do not rebrand teaching × MIS interaction, teaching opportunity costs, or condition-dependent policy differences as new merely because another article motivated them.
* **Campaign 3 / META-VOC:** the repository reports a specific FAST/DEEP/META-VOC result and predictor-validity limitations in its declared synthetic domain. This is adjacent to adaptive deliberation, but neither establishes System-1.x nor a general learned router/MixLLM effect.

The candidate papers can motivate external grounding or a new dependency, but not erase paid-for HLS findings. Any future proposal should specify what new mathematical/causal/informational/organizational dependency cannot already be answered by those results, and define a STOP/reduction condition.

### Acceptance criteria common to all integrations

1. Source version, exact equations, variable definitions, units and domains are pinned.
2. Native mechanism has independent equation/unit tests and reproduces available source benchmarks before adaptation.
3. HLS adapter is explicitly labelled `RELATED` unless equivalence is demonstrated; it preserves the HLS baseline path unchanged.
4. State namespaces distinguish competence, productivity, experience, router parameters, uncertainty, quality, cost, latency and queue state.
5. Mechanism toggles isolate the nominated intervention; no hidden switch changes policy information or candidate action set.
6. Demand and observation streams are recorded and policy-independent where designed; common paths are frozen before policy runs.
7. Resource accounting remains a vector with explicit units. No arbitrary utility conversion; no equating HLS effort with money, waiting or search states.
8. Negative and equivalence controls are prespecified; deterministic replay and output traces are tested.
9. Historical HLS outputs/catalogues/hashes are not edited to make an adapter pass.
10. Findings are scoped to the model/task/domain and are compared against the Historical Memory & Novelty Guard before a campaign.

## 10. Effort summary and final decision

| Article | Current directly reusable HLS infrastructure | Adapter need | Full mechanism support now? | Comparative status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nembhard–Bentefouet | Agents/skills, task requirements, assignment catalogue, additive performance traces, exogenous sequence, resources, MIS control for contrast | Separate experience/productivity state, indirect team experience, selection/grouping/team assignment, source-faithful policies/topology | **No** | Source formalized; adaptation/design possible, faithful HLS reproduction not present |
| Baccara–Lee–Yariv | Generic episode recording, assignment as broad concept, demand replay | Arrivals, queues, service rate/capacity, training technology, wait/welfare/equilibrium | **No** | No current HLS-runner comparison |
| MixLLM | Narrow policy `choose` seam, information view patterns, seeded/pairing and trace conventions | Query/model feature set, quality/cost predictors, router-specific learning/uncertainty, token/queue accounting | **No** | Routing-class comparison plausible after substantial inputs; original not implemented |
| System-1.x | Separate Campaign 3 fast/deep/meta experimental pattern and compute traces | Planning states, subgoals, difficulty, fast/deep plan operators, verifier, search budget and training pipeline | **No** | No current HLS task/domain comparison |

**Final recommendation: REFRAME.** Keep the overall research direction GO for *auditing/model-specific design*, but do not authorize a four-paper integrated laboratory implementation or common ranking. First complete fidelity specifications. Then consider a narrow Nembhard reference/adaptation as the first candidate because its task/worker/experience problem aligns most directly with HLS's ontology and uses existing assignment/environment/trace foundations; it is still an extension, not a direct reuse. If the PI's priority is specifically a router decision rather than organizational learning, MixLLM could instead be the first separately scoped adaptation after query/cost/feedback contracts are established. The present evidence does not justify choosing that alternative by convenience alone.

### Blockers that are real today

* No service queue, waiting time, arrival/service process, or endogenous service capacity for Baccara.
* No plan/subgoal representation, plan validator, or search-state budget for System-1.x.
* No query-specific quality/cost predictors, token price model, model queue, or router learning state for MixLLM.
* No Nembhard direct/indirect experience state, incidental peer transfer, selection/grouping stage, task-class compatibility, or serial/parallel production evaluator.
* No generic statistical campaign runner that makes all resource units/outcomes cross-paper comparable. Existing paired helpers are specific to HLS/each study.

These blockers do not block documentation or a source-specific standalone reference model; they do block claims of current reproduction, integrated comparison, or universal comparison.

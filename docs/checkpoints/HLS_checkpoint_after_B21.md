# HLS Scientific Checkpoint after B2.1

Status: scientific consolidation of repository results through B2.1. It adds
no theory, experiment, novelty claim, or paper claim.

## 1. Research question

RQ0 is reproduced exactly from the canonical research-question document:

> Can the dynamic allocation and development of competences in a
> heterogeneous learning system improve long-term system performance
> compared with architectures that manage task allocation and knowledge
> transfer separately?

Its canonical status remains HYPOTHESIS and sole official research question.

An HLS contains models or agents with heterogeneous competences, costs,
speeds, capacities, and learning responses. It makes two distinct choices:
who does what operationally, and who learns what and from whom. The programme
studies this cycle:

    operation
        -> learning opportunities
        -> competence development
        -> future operational possibilities
        -> operation.

RQ0 asks for strict-advantage, equality, and no-advantage conditions against a
scientifically strong separated architecture. It does not define separation as
weak myopia or treat policy-class inclusion as a substantive result.

## 2. Common ontology

Only the objects needed here are summarized. Canonical definitions remain in
docs/hls_ontology.md.

- \(S\) is competence state. It can be vector-valued and need not be directly
  observable. Competence is not observed quality: \(S\ne\bar Q\ne Q\).
- \(a\) is operational allocation or organization: routing, assignment,
  collaboration, escalation, or another rule for current work.
- \(d\) is a competence-development decision, distinct from \(a\) and from
  the exposure it generates.
- \(E\) is direct or indirect learning-relevant experience, generated by a
  model-specific map \(E(X,a,d,W)\). \(E\ne\Delta S\).
- \(V(S)\) below is future operational or continuation value of competence
  state \(S\), not an identification of competence with value.
- \(\Omega(S,q,a)\) is the incremental continuation value of development
  opportunities available after operational action \(a\).
- \(\Gamma\) is a finite-difference interaction in continuation value.
- \(H(D)=-K(D)+\beta[V(S_D)-V(S)]\) is the full value of development set
  \(D\), including present costs and future value.

The distinctions \(a\ne d\), \(d\ne E\), and \(E\ne\Delta S\) are essential.
Routing can make an opportunity available without using it, and use need not
produce the same competence change for every learner.

## 3. Theoretical results established

All results here are model-scoped DERIVED-IN-MODEL results or exact identities
under their stated assumptions. They are not universal HLS laws.

### 3.1 Equivalence/frontier results

The minimal-model record establishes four important boundaries.

1. Under additive separability,
   \(J=C+V^A a+V^D d\), \(a,d\ge0\), \(a+d\le1\), an optimally coordinated
   separated share attains
   \(J_S^*=J_J^*=C+\max\{V^A,V^D\}\).
2. With simple work-dependent exposure \(E^{ind}=ad\), the corrected
   separated best-response set contains the unique joint optimum in the
   stated interior regime. The unresolved issue is equilibrium selection,
   not a strict gap against the best separated equilibrium.
3. Complementary competence value can require nonlocal state information.
   A constant price fails across the derived threshold, while
   \(\tau^*(S_2)=pR\lambda S_2\) reproduces the joint choice exactly.
4. At \(T=3\), the correct price depends on an explicit continuation-policy
   threshold. A continuation-aware price or rich nonlinear contract remains
   sufficient in the recorded model.

Thus the programme has not shown that joint HLS is superior to every
sufficiently rich separated architecture. Physical separation, decision
objectives, information, computation, and coordination are distinct.

### 3.2 Opportunity-generation mechanism

The exact decomposition is

\[
Q(S,q,a)=R(S,q,a)+\beta V(S)+\Omega(S,q,a),
\]

and the explicit no-use action gives \(\Omega\ge0\). Therefore

\[
a^*\in\arg\max_a\{R(S,q,a)+\Omega(S,q,a)\}.
\]

Current operation can change which development opportunities are available.
Whether \(\Omega\) depends on \(a\) and whether development-set value is
additive are separate structural questions.

### 3.3 Portfolio interaction

For competence changes \(\Delta_i,\Delta_j\),

\[
\Gamma_{ij}=V(S+\Delta_i+\Delta_j)-V(S+\Delta_i)
-V(S+\Delta_j)+V(S).
\]

Positive values denote complementarity, negative values substitution, and
zero modularity at those four states. Increasing or decreasing differences
connect the sign to the geometry of \(V\).

### 3.4 Operationally induced interaction

Independent developments can interact solely because of future operation.

- One scarce Deep backup shared across two future classes gives
  \(\Gamma_{\rm SUB}\le0\).
- A Fast task limited by \(\min\{s_1,s_2\}\), with Deep alternative \(h\),
  gives \(\Gamma_{\rm COMP}\ge0\).
- A mixed workload has
  \(\Gamma(p)=p\Gamma_{\rm SUB}+(1-p)\Gamma_{\rm COMP}\).
- For additive policies with \(V=\max\{V^A,V^B\}\), the exact interaction is

\[
\Gamma(z,x,y)=[z+x+y]_+-[z+x]_+-[z+y]_++[z]_+.
\]

It is nonnegative for \(xy>0\), nonpositive for \(xy<0\), and zero if either
increment vanishes or one policy remains optimal at all four vertices.

### 3.5 Integrated opportunity + portfolio result

Proposition 7 combines endogenous opportunity availability and complementary
continuation value. For \(s_1=s_2=s>h\) and
\(\Delta_1=\Delta_2=\Delta\),

\[
H_i=-(s-h)-\kappa_i<0,
\]

\[
H_{12}=-2(s-h)-\kappa_1-\kappa_2+\beta\Delta.
\]

Hence \(H_{12}>0\) exactly when

\[
\beta\Delta>2(s-h)+\kappa_1+\kappa_2,
\]

and \(H_{12}=H_1+H_2+\beta\Gamma_{12}\), with
\(\Gamma_{12}=\Delta\). \(\Omega\) is needed because operation determines
opportunity availability; \(\Gamma\) is needed because joint continuation
value differs from the sum of individual values. They are distinct mechanisms.

### 3.6 Generalization

Propositions 8–9 define

\[
\Xi_V(D)=V(S_D)-V(S)-\sum_{i\in D}[V(S_i)-V(S)],
\]

\[
\Xi_K(D)=K(D)-\sum_{i\in D}K_i,
\]

and give the exact identity

\[
H(D)=\sum_{i\in D}H_i+\beta\Xi_V(D)-\Xi_K(D).
\]

Independent developments, additive costs, and modular continuation value form
a modular frontier: aggregation cannot rescue individually non-beneficial
opportunities. Uniform nonnegative cross-partials on the reachable rectangle
give

\[
\Xi_V(D)\ge\sum_{i<j}\mu_{ij}\Delta_i\Delta_j.
\]

Exceeding accumulated individual deficits is sufficient, not necessary, for
\(H(D)>0\). A finite-difference version covers non-smooth values. For more
than two opportunities, higher-order terms remain possible; base-state pair
interactions do not generally determine \(\Xi_V(D)\).

## 4. Experimental evidence

### 4.1 B1

B1 used digits, LogisticRegression as Fast, RandomForest as Deep, four
competence clusters learned from validation confusion profiles, and factorial
pseudo-label interventions. Models were near ceiling: mean test balanced
accuracy was 0.9533 Fast and 0.9652 Deep. Cluster stability was low
(ARI mean 0.1330, range -0.1638 to 0.6828). Mean \(\Gamma\) by budget was
0.000324, -0.000388, -0.000825, and -0.000458; both signs occurred and
variability exceeded the means. The final classification was
OBSERVED — WEAK / UNSTABLE INTERACTION.

Unstable learned regions and near-ceiling performance motivated PACS domains
as fixed, a-priori axes for measuring competence profiles with greater
headroom.

### 4.2 B2.0 PACS calibration

B2.0 audited 9,991 PACS images and fixed photo, art_painting, cartoon, and
sketch as the four axes of the declared competence profile. Validation BA
operationalizes the score on each axis; it does not identify latent competence
\(S\) with observed quality. Fast was ImageNet-pretrained MobileNetV2; Deep was
ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50; five stratified split seeds were retained. The
valid calibration run used CPU. MPS was excluded after diagnosis of label
corruption on a non-blocking CPU-to-MPS transfer.

For the 25% Fast state later used by B2.1, mean validation domain BA was
0.895769, 0.733265, 0.819304, and 0.719560; Deep means were 0.947510,
0.743523, 0.794039, and 0.761952 in the same domain order. B2.1 fixed
\(F_0\) to 25% of BASE. This is distinct from the earlier automatic B2.0 rule
output: stored B2.0 summary.json records 10% and a subsequent TEST evaluation.
TEST was closed during design/selection, and B2.1 never used it.

### 4.3 B2.1 factorial learning

For every seed and \(N\in\{25,50,100\}\), B2.1 started every intervention
from the same 25%-BASE \(F_0\). Deep supplied hard pseudo-labels from only the
relevant TRANSFER domain. The four states were \(F_0,F_i,F_j,F_{ij}\), with
\(F_{ij}\) trained directly from \(F_0\) on the \(N+N\) mixture. There were
five seeds and six domain pairs. All scores used VALIDATION; TEST stayed closed.

All 18 \(N\times\)pair mean \(\Gamma_{learn}\) values were positive. At
\(N=25\), three pairs were positive in 5/5 seeds; at \(N=50\) and 100, all
six pairs were positive in 5/5 seeds. No pair was consistently substitutive.
Mean \(\Gamma_{learn}\) increased with \(N\) for every pair. The strongest
means involved sketch:

| Pair | N=25 | N=50 | N=100 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| photo-sketch | 0.092427 | 0.271910 | 0.429410 |
| art_painting-sketch | 0.062488 | 0.234806 | 0.390827 |
| cartoon-sketch | 0.062470 | 0.200663 | 0.366430 |

These are interactions among learned states. Their size is consistent with
nonlinear optimization, interference, or forgetting during short fine-tuning,
but B2.1 did not identify the responsible learning dynamic. They are not by
themselves evidence of routing-induced interaction.

### 4.4 Operational transformation

The same learned states were valued by

\[
V_{\rm oper}(F;c)=\sum_k p_k\max\{S_k(F),S_k(D)-c\},\qquad p_k=1/4.
\]

At \(c=0\), mean \(\Gamma_{learn}=0.166432\) and mean
\(\Gamma_{oper}=0.012528\). At \(c=0.15\), mean
\(\Gamma_{oper}=0.091474\). Routing changed relative to \(F_0\) in at least
one factorial state in 420/450 observations. \(\Delta\Gamma\) moved toward
zero as \(c\) increased.

Mean \(\Delta\Gamma\) ranged from -0.078605 for
route_interaction_count 0 to -0.199394 for count 4. There were 20/450
amplifications and 21/450 sign changes. At seed 3, \(N=25\),
cartoon-sketch, \(c=0.10\), \(\Gamma_{learn}=-0.005327\) and
\(\Gamma_{oper}=0.029819\). Maximum absorption was seed 3, \(N=100\),
photo-sketch, \(c=0\), with \(\Delta\Gamma=-0.448450\).

Operational organization therefore transforms interaction among already
learned competence states. A cheap Deep alternative tends to absorb
interaction by substituting for Fast where Fast loses competence; increasing
Deep cost makes \(\Gamma_{oper}\) approach \(\Gamma_{learn}\). This association
is descriptive, not causal identification.

## 5. Theory ↔ experiment map

| Theoretical construct | Theoretical status | Experimental realization | Empirical status | What is still missing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(S\) | Canonical competence state | Four fixed PACS-domain axes, measured by validation BA | Declared proxy observed on VALIDATION | Other tasks and competence measures |
| \(\Delta S\) | Change produced by \(L(S,E)\) | Scores of \(F_i,F_j,F_{ij}\) relative to \(F_0\) | Observed descriptively | Identification of the learning mechanism |
| \(\Gamma\) | Exact finite difference of \(V\) | \(\Gamma_{learn}\) over four factorial states | Strong positive VALIDATION interactions | Generalization and inferential evidence |
| \(V(S)\) | Model-scoped continuation value | Uniform mean BA and Fast/Deep maximum | Evaluated for declared functions | Application-grounded value and horizon |
| routing | Operational decision \(a\) | Domain-level Fast/Deep choice for each \(c\) | 420/450 cases changed | Causal intervention on routing |
| \(\Omega\) | Exact opportunity-value term | Not instantiated | Not empirically tested | operation to learning-opportunity evidence |
| \(H(D)\) | Exact opportunity-set decomposition | Costs and opportunity generation absent | Not empirically tested | Costs, discounting, endogenous opportunities |

B2.1 supports the partial chain:

    learning -> competence changes -> V(S)
             -> operational routing -> Gamma_oper.

It does not test operation to learning opportunity. The full HLS cycle is not
experimentally closed.

## 6. What we can / cannot claim

### CAN CLAIM

- Exact model-scoped identities and boundaries exist for opportunity
  generation, portfolio interaction, operationally induced interaction, and
  finite-set opportunity value.
- Rich separated coordination matches the joint optimum in the recorded
  equivalence/frontier models.
- B2.1 VALIDATION produced strong, broadly positive \(\Gamma_{learn}\),
  especially for pairs containing sketch.
- The declared Fast/Deep operational value substantially transformed these
  interactions across the predeclared cost grid.

### CANNOT CLAIM

- Novelty has not been completely audited.
- Joint HLS is superior to every rich separated architecture.
- Routing causally produced \(\Delta\Gamma\).
- B2.1 generalizes to TEST.
- A complete HLS policy is superior.
- \(\Omega\) has empirical validation.
- The full closed-loop HLS cycle has empirical validation.

## 7. Current scientific position

There is now a non-trivial theoretical base for interaction among operation,
competence development, and future value. Negative results delimit what cannot
support joint-versus-separated superiority. PACS B2.1 shows that real learned
competence developments can interact strongly and that Fast/Deep organization
substantially transforms those interactions. The experimentally missing link
is operation to learning opportunity; the full cycle is not yet closed.

\section{Theory of competence orchestration: current scaffold}

This section records \textbf{CANDIDATE / WORKING HYPOTHESES}, not established theory. It is a scaffold for an integration theory that remains under development.

\paragraph{Dual value of operational allocation.} A work-allocation action can have immediate service value and experience or knowledge-generation value. The latter need not be present in every system, but it is the candidate link between current operation and future competence.

\paragraph{System value of learning.} Teaching a competence should not conceptually be valued only by local learner improvement. Its value may depend on learnability, expected future use, portfolio role, learning cost, and, where a physical transition model warrants it, interference or forgetting.

\paragraph{Portfolio organization rather than individual maximization.} The intended objective is not assumed to be $\max\sum_{i,z}c_{iz}$. Depending on environment and constraints, a competence portfolio may make specialization, broadening, replication, or rebalancing conditionally useful. No general preference among these families is established.

\paragraph{Multiple time scales.} Operational routing can be fast, teaching and learning intermediate, and portfolio evolution slow. This is a conceptual distinction, not an assertion of a strict universal scale separation or numerical time constants.

\subsection*{Candidate dynamic response}
Under a persistent environmental change, a candidate architecture-level prediction is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{environmental change} &\rightarrow \text{competence mismatch} \rightarrow \text{teacher buffering}\\
&\rightarrow \text{selective learning} \rightarrow \text{portfolio reorganization}\\
&\rightarrow \text{new division of labour}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:candidate-response}
\end{equation}
Teacher buffering means that a capable role can maintain service while the less costly layer adapts and can simultaneously provide potentially reusable knowledge. Equation~\eqref{eq:candidate-response} is a hypothesis for future operationalization, not a theorem or evidence that adaptation is superior under drift.

\paragraph{Trade-offs to develop.} The candidate trade-offs are current efficiency versus future capability; specialization versus coverage or redundancy; teacher exploitation versus teacher-enabled learning; and reactive adaptation versus anticipatory competence investment. The required transition models, objectives, and conditions have not yet been established.

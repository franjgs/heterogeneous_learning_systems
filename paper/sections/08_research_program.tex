\section{Experimental methodology: validation programme}

General HLS experiments have not yet been conducted. This section specifies comparison classes and observables for future validation; it reports no dataset, benchmark, or empirical outcome.

The required comparison classes are: a single expensive expert; a heterogeneous portfolio with static or frozen competence; a static hierarchy with strong routing; a hierarchy with reactive or local learning; and a deliberate competence-evolution HLS. Where applicable, comparisons must match models, data, compute, learning or training budget, and policy information. A weak router or extra training capacity is not evidence for deliberate evolution.

Two regimes should be distinguished. In a \emph{static regime}, $E_t$ is approximately stationary. In a \emph{dynamic regime}, relevant environmental elements change, such as demand, task distribution, cost, latency requirements, resources, constraints, or required competences. The candidate teacher-buffering and competence-reorganization response described in the orchestration scaffold is to be tested rather than assumed.

Candidate observables are quality, operational cost, latency, teacher utilization, adaptation cost, cumulative long-horizon utility, competence distribution or organization, and recovery or adaptation time after change. Every experiment must separately record construct validity, causal identification, comparison validity, and external validity. Controlled synthetic experiments can isolate a mechanism; realistic evaluations can test relevance. Neither alone establishes RQ0.
